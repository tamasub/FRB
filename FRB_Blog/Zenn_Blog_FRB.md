# FRB (Fishing Rod Benchmark): Measuring Fishing Rod Sensitivity as Vibration

**FRB (Fishing Rod Benchmark)** is a personal research project that attempts to compare and visualize **fishing rod sensitivity** as vibration.

Fishing rod sensitivity has traditionally been described through subjective words:

- sensitive
- dull
- informative
- sharp
- rich
- responsive

These words are useful, but they are difficult to share.

Two anglers may both say, “This rod is sensitive,” while feeling completely different things.

FRB starts from one question:

> Can fishing rod sensitivity be treated as a reproducible vibration structure?

---

## Declaration

I declare the creation and development of:

> **Fishing Rod Benchmark (FRB)**  
> a proposed common framework for observing, comparing, and sharing fishing rod sensitivity as vibration.

The goal is not to decide which rod is “better.”

The goal is to create a **shared language** for discussing the differences between rods.

FRB is not only a measurement method.

It is also an attempt to build a culture where anglers can share what they feel.

---

## What FRB Measures

FRB focuses on the vibration that reaches the human body through a fishing rod.

In this project, sensitivity is treated as:

> **the structure of vibration that a human can perceive.**

Sensitivity is not simply vibration strength.

A rod that vibrates strongly is not always easier to read.

A rod that produces many frequency peaks is not always more informative.

FRB tries to observe:

- what kind of vibration appears
- which frequency bands respond
- how stable or unstable the vibration structure is
- how the human body perceives that structure
- how differences between rods can be shared

In short:

> **Sensitivity is not just “how much it vibrates.”  
> Sensitivity is how vibration becomes recognizable information.**

---

## Why This Matters

In many fishing conversations, rod sensitivity is treated as personal experience.

That is natural.

Fishing is a physical activity, and the human body is part of the system.

However, this also creates a problem.

When sensitivity is described only by personal feeling, it becomes difficult to compare, reproduce, or share.

FRB tries to bridge that gap.

It does not remove human feeling from the equation.

Instead, it places human perception at the center.

> FRB does not replace human feeling.  
> FRB tries to make human feeling shareable.

---

## Basic Concept

FRB uses controlled vibration inputs and observes the response of fishing rods.

The current approach includes:

- indoor reproducible vibration tests
- ESP32-based vibration measurement
- FFT analysis
- visual comparison of vibration structures
- human perception notes
- AI-assisted interpretation

The current system is experimental, but the direction is clear:

> Use reproducible vibration inputs to compare the vibration structures that humans actually feel.

---

## Phase 1 — Surface Response

Phase 1 observes how a rod responds to continuous contact vibration.

The simplest example is rubbing the rod tip or line against a material under controlled conditions.

Different materials produce different vibration tendencies.

For example:

- carpet-like input: low-frequency response
- flooring-like input: mid-frequency response
- stainless-like input: high-frequency response

This is not meant to be a perfect laboratory standard.

It is meant to be a practical indoor method for comparing how rods respond to different vibration bands.

The key idea is:

> If the input can be reproduced indoors, the difference between rods becomes easier to observe.

---

## Phase 2 — Simulated Bite Response

Phase 2 focuses on changes in line tension.

A fish bite can be understood physically as a force change on the line.

Examples include:

- **Impulse** — a short impact, like a “tap”
- **Suction** — a pull-in or load change
- **Weed** — interference or dragging resistance

FRB attempts to reproduce these inputs indoors using simple structures such as rubber bands, springs, and controlled tension release.

This allows bite-like vibration responses to be tested without needing to be at the sea.

---

## Phase 3 — Perception Training

FRB is not only about rods.

It is also about the human side.

Even if a vibration exists, the angler may not recognize it.

FRB therefore treats indoor vibration experience as a form of **perception training**.

The goal is to build an internal “vibration dictionary.”

For example, by experiencing controlled indoor vibrations, an angler may become better at recognizing:

- sand
- rock
- weed
- line rubbing
- subtle residual vibration
- small “puru-puru” vibration

FRB calls this:

> **Perception training for feeling the sea.**

Indoor tests are not a replacement for real fishing.

They are a way to prepare the body and brain to notice what is happening outside.

---

## Difference Culture

FRB is based on the idea of looking at differences.

The goal is not ranking.

The goal is comparison for understanding.

For example:

- before modification vs after modification
- one contact condition vs another
- one vibration input vs another
- one rod structure vs another
- one perceived experience vs another

When a difference becomes visible, people naturally begin to ask:

> Why did it change?

That question creates hypotheses.

Hypotheses lead to experiments.

Experiments lead to shared experience.

This is what I call **difference culture**.

FRB aims to grow a culture where anglers can enjoy looking at differences together.

---

## Rod Modification Difference Culture

FRB also observes differences created by rod modification.

This does not mean ranking commercial rods or judging manufacturers.

It means observing how a specific rod changes after a specific action.

Examples:

- adding copper tape
- changing a guide
- changing grip structure
- adding carbon material
- changing contact points
- reducing unnecessary vibration

The comparison is:

> before = baseline  
> after = comparison target

The purpose is not to say “this modification is universally better.”

The purpose is to say:

> This change produced this kind of vibration difference.

That difference can then be shared.

---

## What FRB Is Not

FRB is often misunderstood, so I want to clarify the boundaries.

FRB is not:

- a ranking system for fishing rods
- a way to decide which rod is best
- a replacement for manufacturer specifications
- a cast performance benchmark
- a rod power or action measurement
- a bending curve measurement
- a static stiffness test
- a catch-rate measurement
- an AI automatic scoring system
- a benchmark for phishing detection
- the Quantum “Benchmark” fishing gear series
- Fast Radio Burst research

FRB is:

> a proposed framework for comparing and sharing the vibration structures that humans perceive as fishing rod sensitivity.

## Why AI Is Involved

This project is being developed through human-AI collaboration.

The experiments are physical.

The rod is real.

The vibration is real.

The human perception is real.

But AI helps in several ways:

- organizing observations
- finding hypotheses
- naming phenomena
- structuring articles
- comparing differences
- keeping the project moving

In that sense, FRB is not only a fishing project.

It is also an example of how AI can expand human thinking.

---

## Current Status

FRB is currently in an experimental and validation phase.

It is not an official industry standard.

It is not a completed specification.

The methods, terms, and scores are still evolving.

However, one thing has become clear through repeated experiments:

> Rod differences can be observed as vibration differences.

And those vibration differences can sometimes match human perception.

That is the starting point of FRB.

---

## Vision

FRB aims to create a world where anglers can say not only:

> “This rod is sensitive.”

but also:

> “This rod has this kind of vibration structure.”  
> “This rod makes this kind of information easier to feel.”  
> “This vibration feels like sand.”  
> “This vibration feels like rock.”  
> “This difference is worth sharing.”

The final goal is not measurement itself.

The final goal is culture.

> FRB is a culture for making the underwater world more perceivable, more discussable, and more shareable.
